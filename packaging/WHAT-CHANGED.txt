Order book trading
==================

NEW PACKAGE  guessmarket.engine.orderbook
    OrderSide     Buy or Sell
    Order         one instruction, with what is left of it
    OrderBook     the resting orders for one option, plus LAST BID ASK MID
                  SPREAD. Bids are kept highest first, asks lowest first, and
                  orders at the same price keep their arrival order
    Trade         one completed piece of business, a match or a mint
    MatchResult   what an order caused: the trades, who owes what, and how much
                  is still resting. The event works it out, the engine applies
                  it, because an event does not know about accounts

CHANGED
    Event            holds one book per option, and does the matching and the
                     minting
    EventStateDto    carries the books, the trades and the base value
    engine           placeOrder(event, user, option, side, quantity, price)
    events screen    a Place order button on order book events, replacing Buy
                     shares, which now only appears on LMSR events
    book panes       show the five statistics and the resting orders, and the
                     trade list underneath

HOW MATCHING WORKS
    A buy order takes the cheapest asks first, a sell order the highest bids,
    and one order may consume several. The order that was already waiting sets
    the price, as an exchange does. Nobody trades with themselves.

HOW MINTING WORKS
    Only when the event allows it, and only between two buy orders on opposite
    options whose prices together reach the base value d.
    The order already waiting pays its own price. The order that has just
    arrived pays whatever completes d. So the event account always receives
    exactly d for each new pair, however far the two prices overshoot.
    The quantity is the smaller of the two.

MONEY
    An order is only accepted if the user can cover it, counting what they
    already have tied up in orders still resting. So a match can never fail
    part way through.
    An on-purchase commission goes straight to the market maker, as the course
    simulation does it. An on-close commission is taken from the winners.

CHECKED ON PAPER, BEFORE ANY OF IT RAN
    Appendix B's own example: a buy of 50 against resting sells of 20 and 40
    takes 20 then 30, leaving 10. Correct.
    The simulation's mint: buy 35 YES at 0.58 and 35 NO at 0.42 puts exactly
    35.00 into the event account, and each side pays 20.30 and 14.70. Correct.
    A mint at 0.60 and 0.55 has the resting side pay 0.60 and the new side pay
    0.40, so the account still receives exactly d per pair. Correct.
    A whole event, opened, traded, minted and closed, conserves money to the
    penny on both commission methods, and the event account ends at zero.
